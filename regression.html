No Health Insurance and Mental Distress in NYC ZIP Codes

Regression Table

Understanding the Results

The correlation is +0.65, which shows a moderate positive relationship between not having health insurance and mental distress.

For every 1 percentage point increase in adults without health insurance, the predicted percentage of adults experiencing mental distress increases by about 0.2768 percentage points.

The p-value is less than 0.001, which is below 0.05. This means the relationship is statistically significant.

What This Means for People

In simple words, this shows that NYC ZIP codes with more adults who do not have health insurance also tend to have more adults experiencing mental distress. I think this is important because having access to healthcare is a major public health concern, and understanding these patterns can help researchers identify communities that may need more support. However, the data do not prove that not having health insurance directly causes mental distress.
