Cross-Sectional Data and Anxiety Trends

The researcher concluded that as young adults moved through their twenties over the past decade, their anxiety became worse. They said this showed that a generation was becoming more anxious as it aged. However, the way the study was done does not fully support the claim.

Every year since 2014, researchers surveyed a new random sample of about 27,000 adults across the country. Each person completed the survey only once and answered questions about their mental health symptoms during the past 30 days. The percentage of adults ages 18 to 25 reporting frequent anxiety symptoms increased from 8.1% in 2014 to 14.7% in 2023.

The problem is that the researchers did not follow the same people over time. A different group of adults was surveyed each year. Because of this, they cannot say that one generation became more anxious as they got older. The results only show that adults aged 18 to 25 reported more anxiety in 2023 than adults in the same age group did in 2014. Also, the way the survey was conducted changed in 2019, which could have affected people's answers.

In general, cross-sectional data can show how common a health issue is at a certain time and can show differences between groups. However, it cannot show how the same people change over time or prove that one thing caused another.

A fair conclusion would be: frequent anxiety symptoms were more common among adults ages 18 to 25 in 2023 than among adults of the same age in 2014.
